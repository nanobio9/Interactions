Discontinuity in Slope of the Radial Green Function

Consider the “exterior” Dirichlet problem: the potential outside a grounded conducting sphere of radius \(a\). Its Green function can be written down directly with the method of images (Jackson, Section 2.6). Expanding both the point-source term and its image in spherical harmonics gives Jackson’s Eq. (3.114):

\[ G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')=4\pi\sum_{l,m}\frac{1}{2l+1}\,g_l(r,r')\,Y^*_{lm}(\theta',\phi')\,Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi), \]

where the radial Green function, Eq. (3.115), can be written in one line:

\[ g_l(r,r')=\frac{r_<^l}{r_>^{l+1}}-\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{a^2}{r\,r'}\right)^{l+1}=\frac{1}{r_>}\left(\frac{r_<}{r_>}\right)^{l}\left[1-\left(\frac{a}{r_<}\right)^{2l+1}\right]. \]

For a fixed source radius \(r'\), \(g_l\) vanishes on the sphere \(r=a\), decays as \(r\to\infty\), and is continuous at \(r=r'\). Its slope, however, jumps there:

\[ \left.\frac{\partial g_l}{\partial r}\right|_{r=r'^+}-\left.\frac{\partial g_l}{\partial r}\right|_{r=r'^-}=-\frac{2l+1}{r'^2}, \]

independent of \(a\). This kink is the radial trace of the point source: it is exactly what the \(\delta(r-r')\) on the right-hand side of the radial equation demands. Choose \(l\) and \(r'\) below (lengths in units of \(a\)) and watch the two one-sided tangents at \(r=r'\).
