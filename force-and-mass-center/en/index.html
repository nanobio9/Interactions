Force-Center and Center-of-Mass Frames

In a two-body gravitational system, both bodies orbit their common center of mass—not each other, and not a fixed point. The equivalent one-body description tracks only the relative coordinate \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2\), which always traces a clean conic section regardless of the mass ratio. Use the sliders below to see what the same orbit looks like from two different vantage points.

When we reduce the two-body central-force problem to an equivalent one-body problem, students often assume this simplification requires one mass to be much larger than the other. It does not. The center-of-mass coordinate \(\mathbf{R}\) drops out of the Lagrangian because it is cyclic—a consequence of the structure of the kinetic energy alone, independent of any mass ratio. We use the COM frame because the math tells us to, not because \(m_2 \gg m_1\).
